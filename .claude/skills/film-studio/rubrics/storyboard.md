# Storyboard rubric

For `work/storyboard.json` and its proof sheet in `work/stills/`. Reviewers also read
`work/script.json`, `work/brief.json`, the platform file, and the film's `style.md`.
Score this rubric AND the style's own `styles/<style>/rubric.md` (a second scorecard with
`stage: "style"`). Both must pass. Protocol and gate: `rubrics/review-protocol.md`.

| # | Parameter | 9-10 looks like | 6 or below when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cover frame and hook | Panel one stops a scroll as a still image and sits inside the platform's hook window. | A plain title card, or the hook arrives after the window. |
| 2 | One focal point | Every panel has one thing to look at and one thing to read; the eye knows where to go. | Competing elements, or text and picture saying two different things. |
| 3 | Script fidelity | Each scene's panels show its `visual_intent`; on-screen words come from the script; the turn gets the peak panel. | Panels illustrate words instead of meaning, or the turn is visually flat. |
| 4 | Layout choice | Each layout is the right tool (number as `stat`, comparison as `versus`, sequence as `timeline`); layouts vary. | Everything is a `title`; three of one layout in a row; a chart with no real data. |
| 5 | Readability | All text is readable at phone size on the sheet; headlines are 2-6 words; nothing is clipped or crowded. | Small text, long lines, text over busy areas. |
| 6 | Platform fit | Content sits inside the safe area; panel lengths match the platform's pace; captions are present and short. | Text under the interface zones, captions missing, or the pace of another platform. |
| 7 | Rhythm | Panel lengths follow information load; dense panels are followed by simple ones; energy rises to one peak per scene. | Metronome pacing, or `peak` spent on setup. |
| 8 | Cast used well | Poses and expressions act the line; characters face each other or the subject; props appear when they make the point. | Idle figures as decoration, or cast missing from scenes about them. |
| 9 | Numbers honest | Every figure on screen is in the script's facts, labelled, with units; charts start at zero and are not embellished. | A number the script does not support, or an unlabelled or rounded-up figure. |
| 10 | Ending and loop | The last panel lands the idea visually; on loop platforms it cuts cleanly to panel one. | A generic outro, or a last frame that fights the first. |

## How to check
- Look at the sheet before reading the JSON. For each panel ask: what is this about? If you cannot
  say in five words, parameter 2 is under 9.
- Parameter 3: read the script's `visual_intent` for each scene, then look at that scene's panels.
- Parameter 5: judge at the sheet's size. It is roughly a phone held at arm's length.
- Parameter 9: for each number on the sheet, find it in `script.json`.
- The sheet freezes each panel at 85% of its length; entrances are finished. Judge motion from the
  style's `style.md`, not from the still.
- Evidence always cites a panel id (`s2p1`).
